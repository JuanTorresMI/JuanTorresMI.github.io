Craft Ledger

Which Minecraft crafts actually make money on your server

Craft Ledger is a free crafting profit calculator for SMP economies. Enter the prices your server trades at and it ranks every vanilla recipe by what it really pays, counting each ingredient at what you could have sold it for instead. Built on DonutSMP; works on any server with an auction house, order board or player shops.

Open Craft Ledger How to use it on DonutSMP

What it tells you

Every SMP economy has the same question under it: is it worth crafting this, or should I just sell the materials? Craft Ledger answers that for all 2,770 craftable items at once. You never enter a recipe. The full vanilla recipe set is built in, read from the game's own data files, so the more raw materials you price, the more of the list fills in on its own.

For each craft it shows the cost to make, the price it sells for, profit per unit, margin, and total profit at the volume you actually intend to move. Click any row for the cost tree, which shows exactly where every coin of the cost comes from, and a batch calculator that walks the order book instead of assuming the top price holds forever.

How to use it on DonutSMP

  1. Open the ledger and go to My prices. Type an item name and enter the price it trades at on the auction house or order board. Buy and sell are one number, because you fill on orders. Start with the raw materials you handle most: logs, ingots, leather, paper, sugar cane.
  2. Set the fee. If the server takes a cut of every sale, enter it as a percent in the Fee box at the top. It comes off every profit figure.
  3. Pick your fuel. Fuel is a dropdown, not a price. Choose what you burn and every smelt is charged from that fuel's burn time and the price you gave it. A blast furnace or smoker gets sixteen items from one coal, a plain furnace eight, so the ledger charges them differently.
  4. Read Opportunities. Set Volume to the number of units you mean to move. An item whose best order wants nine units at a silly price looks like the best trade on the board at a volume of one and vanishes at a volume of a thousand. Sort by total profit at your real volume and the thing worth doing is on top.
  5. Check Coverage. It lists the items you have not priced, ranked by how many craftable items each one would unlock. Price the top of that list and the biggest part of the tree comes alive at once.

Prices go stale. Every price is stamped when you set it; anything past a week turns amber and past a month turns red, and a stale price deep in a chain quietly skews everything above it. Refresh the ones that matter before you commit to a large batch.

Other servers it works on

Nothing in it is tied to one server. The prices are yours to set, so it works anywhere players trade in a currency:

  • Lifesteal and survival SMPs with an auction house (DonutSMP and servers built like it). Use the going order prices.
  • Servers with chest shops or admin shops. Use the shop's buy price for what you buy and its sell price for what you sell; switch an item's basis to Cheapest if buying is cheaper than making.
  • Private SMPs with a player-run economy. Use whatever your friends have agreed on. The ledger is a good way to notice when a price is off.
  • Skyblock-style servers with a bazaar. Vanilla recipes only, so custom items and modified recipes are not covered, but the plain crafting and smelting chains are.

The one hard limit: it knows the vanilla recipe set and nothing else. A server that changes recipes or adds custom items will not have those in the list.

A worked example

Say books sell for 2,000 on your server and a bookshelf sells for 15,000. It costs 1,161 to craft a book from paper and leather, so the tempting sum is six planks plus three books at 1,161, about 3,600, against 15,000. That is not what the bookshelf costs you. If you can sell a book for 2,000, putting it into a bookshelf costs you 2,000, because you gave up the sale.

  • 6 oak planks at 20: 120
  • 3 books at their 2,000 order price: 6,000
  • Bookshelf costs 6,120 and sells for 15,000
  • Profit on the bookshelf step: 8,880

That is Market basis, the default: every component is counted at what you could have sold it for. Switch the book to Cheapest and the same bookshelf reads 3,603 cost and 11,397 profit. Both are right; they answer different questions. Market tells you whether the last step is worth doing. Cheapest tells you whether the whole chain beats doing nothing. A recipe can pass one and fail the other, and the ledger shows you which.

Questions people ask

Cost

Is it free?

Yes. No account, no sign-up, no ads. It is a page on this site.

Privacy

Where do my prices go?

Nowhere. Everything is stored in your own browser. Export writes a file you keep; Import reads one back. Nothing is sent to a server, including this one.

Prices

Does it know my server's prices?

No. Prices differ on every server and change every week, so it only uses what you enter. If you run a scanner that writes prices to a file, Link scanner reads that file so you never type them at all.

Recipes

Which recipes are included?

Every vanilla recipe in the current game version: crafting, stonecutting, smelting, blasting, smoking, campfire cooking and smithing, plus every item group a recipe accepts. Custom server recipes are not included.

Phone

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Add it to your home screen from the browser's share menu and it opens like an app, with your prices kept on the device.
