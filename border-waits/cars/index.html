How it works

{% if b.ports %}Copied {{ b.as_of_en }}{% endif %}

At every land port, CBP officers post how long each line is, about once an hour, to the agency’s Border Wait Times site: the car lanes (standard, SENTRI or NEXUS, and Ready Lane) and the pedestrian lanes (standard and Ready Lane). It’s the best public number there is, but it’s only ever the number right now. This board keeps every reading, so it can say what a crossing is usually like on a Friday at 6 PM.

  1. A Cloudflare Worker reads CBP every half hour. The same small Worker behind the truck wait board reads CBP’s public feed (no key needed) and keeps each port’s car and pedestrian readings once, with the time the port posted them, in the port’s own clock.
  2. Each reading is kept for good. The Worker holds the last two weeks; every time the site is rebuilt they’re added to its permanent archive, CSV files in this site’s repository (below). Nothing is kept anywhere else.
  3. The record reaches back before the Worker. CBP keeps no past readings. The Worker began keeping these lanes on October 20, 2026; the readings from September 13 to then come from an independent public archive that saved CBP’s whole feed about once an hour (gptcbp/border-crossing-data), read with the same code. That archive missed some hours, so those weeks rest on fewer readings.
  4. Unknown is not zero. When lanes are closed or the update is pending, CBP posts no wait. Those are stored as blanks, not as zero minutes, and averages skip them. A lane a crossing doesn’t have is left blank too.
  5. The averages are the last 30 days. For each crossing, each lane, each day of the week and each hour, the pages show the average posted wait, once a lane has at least two days of readings. Until then they show CBP’s own published averages for {{ b.month_en | default: "this month" }}, from the year before, labeled as CBP’s.
  6. The pages are rebuilt at least once a day, so every number is in the HTML. The “right now” columns also refresh from CBP’s feed when the page opens: your browser asks CBP directly.

How to read it: Cars is the general lanes for passenger vehicles. SENTRI (on the Mexican border) and NEXUS (on the Canadian) are CBP’s trusted-traveler programs, with lanes of their own. The Ready Lane is for travelers whose every passenger carries an RFID-enabled document. Walking is the pedestrian line. The wait is CBP’s, rounded the way CBP rounds it; “no wait” is CBP’s “no delay”.

Source: U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Border Wait Times; readings from September 13 to October 20, 2026 as saved by gptcbp/border-crossing-data. The numbers are public; any mistakes in presenting them are mine. The Worker’s code is in the worker folder of this site’s repository.
