What to price next

Items you have not priced that no recipe can reach either. The number is how many more craftable items open up the moment you give it a price.

Every craftable item

A costing tool for crafting in a player market. Enter what the market charges for the things you trade, and it works out what every recipe actually costs you to make and what you clear on it.

Built against Donut SMP, but nothing in it is tied to one server. Any world where you can buy and sell materials will work — the prices are yours to set.

Getting started

  1. Open My prices and enter the order price for everything you trade. Buy and sell are one number, since you fill on orders.
  2. Go to Opportunities. Every recipe you can now cost is ranked by what it pays.
  3. Click any row for the cost breakdown and a batch calculator.

You only ever enter prices. Recipes come from the game, so the more raw materials you price, the more of the list fills in on its own.

What a component really costs you

If you can sell a book for 2,000, then putting that book into a bookshelf costs you 2,000 — not the 1,161 it cost to craft. You gave up the sale. That is the default here, and it is what Market basis means.

Switch an item to Cheapest and it gets costed at whichever is lower, its order price or making it. The same bookshelf then reads 3,603 and 11,397, because the books are priced at what the paper and leather cost rather than at what they would have sold for.

Both are right, they answer different questions. Market tells you whether the last step is worth doing. Cheapest tells you whether the whole chain beats doing nothing. A recipe can pass one and fail the other.

Reading the cost tree

Every recipe detail shows where its cost comes from, branch by branch. A branch stops at anything tagged market, because that is a sale you are giving up and its own ingredients no longer matter. Branches tagged crafted keep opening downward until they reach something you priced.

When a recipe accepts a group of items, like any plank or any log, it picks whichever one is cheapest for you and shows how many alternatives it had.

The fee and fuel controls

Fee is the cut taken off every sale, entered as a percent.

Fuel is a dropdown, not a price. Pick what you actually burn and the cost per item is worked out from that fuel's burn time and whatever you priced it at, so you never divide anything by hand. Price coal at 30 and pick Coal, and a furnace smelt costs 3.75 while a blast furnace smelt costs 1.88.

That gap is real. A furnace spends 200 ticks on an item and a blast furnace or smoker only 100, so one coal does 8 items in a furnace but 16 in either fast one. A campfire burns no fuel at all, so campfire recipes are never charged for it.

If the fuel you picked has no price yet the box says so, and smelting is costed without it. Set it to None if fuel is genuinely free to you.

Where the recipes come from

The full vanilla recipe set, read straight from the game's own data files. That covers crafting, stonecutting, smelting, blasting, smoking, campfire cooking and smithing, along with every item group a recipe can accept.

What it does not know

  • Custom recipes. Only the vanilla recipe set is loaded. Anything your server has added or changed is not in here, and there is no way to add one yet.
  • Whether your orders fill. Every figure assumes you transact at the price you entered. If your sell orders sit unfilled, the real opportunity cost is lower than shown and crafting looks better than the numbers say.
  • Anything that is not a recipe. Mob drops, farming yield and time spent are all outside it. The per-craft figures are the whole story it can tell.

Coverage

The Coverage tab is the map of what is left. Every craftable item sits in one of three states: done means you have priced it and everything it needs, no sell price means you can already cost it but have not said what it sells for, and blocked means something further down is unpriced. Blocked rows show what they are waiting on.

What to price next ranks the missing items by how many craftable items each one would open up. That number is measured, not guessed — it re-runs the whole graph with that one item priced and counts the difference. Price the top of that list and the largest part of the tree comes alive at once.

How old a price is

Every price is stamped when you set it, and the Set column on the prices tab shows its age. Anything past a week turns amber and past a month turns red. Coverage counts the stale ones for you.

This matters more than it sounds. A price you entered three weeks ago is being treated as fact by every recipe that touches it, and one stale entry deep in a chain quietly skews everything above it.

Where your prices live

In this browser, on this device, and nowhere else. Nothing is uploaded. That also means a phone and a laptop keep separate sets, and clearing browser data wipes them.

Use Export for a backup file and Import to load it somewhere else. Worth doing once you have real numbers in.
