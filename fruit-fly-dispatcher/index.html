Fruit Fly Dispatcher

Four brains run the same delivery week with the same truck, the same nose, the same memory. Only the way they decide differs. One is a fruit fly's navigation circuit, wired neuron by neuron from the FlyWire connectome.

Store dots show shelf level (red = empty, sales lost). The colored line is where the truck went today; a hollow ring shows where the truck believes it is after drift. Dashed lines show what each brain intends next. Numbers under each map count from Monday 00:00. Click a map to inspect that brain below.

Inside the fly's decision

Drag the two smells around the compass; pull a handle toward the center to weaken it. The page runs the real FlyWire circuit (47 heading cells, 82 goal cells, 24 steering cells, 12 turning boosters) and finds every heading the fly would settle on.

Steering command at every heading

Across 14 store layouts

Same experiment at every spacing from tightly packed to scattered: 55 stores, clustered or even, 2-day runs, 3 seeds each. Lines are means; bands span the seeds.

What the wiring had to get right

Every direction the fly's circuit uses was derived from FlyWire wiring alone, then checked against something independent. None of the published numbers were fed in.

What is assumed

    FlyWire whole-brain connectome v783, FlyWire Consortium, Princeton University (CC-BY 4.0): Dorkenwald et al. 2024; Schlegel et al. 2024; Matsliah et al. 2024; Eckstein, Bates et al. 2024; Buhmann et al. 2021; Heinrich et al. 2018; Berg et al. 2025. Steering model form: Mussells Pires et al. 2024; Westeinde et al. 2024; Hulse et al. 2021. FC2 normalization: arXiv 2607.18969. Store economics from the 7-Eleven Japan case (Pearson, 2026). Simulation: flyroute, OR-Tools CP-SAT.
